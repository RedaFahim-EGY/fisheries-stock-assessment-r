# Module 03 — Length-frequency distributions

This module treats length-frequency distributions (LFDs) as **sampled observations whose interpretation depends on coverage, selectivity and measurement practice**, rather than as direct representations of a stock.

The worked example uses a reproducible simulated individual-fish dataset spanning 2016–2025, monthly sampling, three spatial areas, commercial and survey sources, and multiple fleet/gear streams.

## Scientific questions

The module asks:

- Are individual length records valid, unique and measured at comparable precision?
- Is sampling intensity comparable across years, months, areas and gears?
- Are apparent modes potentially affected by length heaping or rounding?
- Are small or large fish missing because of biological structure or because the measurement scope was truncated?
- Do spatial differences persist within gear, and do gear differences persist within area?
- How much does changing fleet/gear/area composition affect a pooled annual LFD?
- What is gained and lost by using histograms, heatmaps and smoothed density plots?
- Which apparent temporal changes remain after stratification or composition standardization?

## Deliberate data problems

The simulation includes scientifically realistic complications:

- reduced commercial coverage in the East in 2019;
- no East-longline observations in April–June 2020;
- 5-cm heaping in part of the 2020–2021 gillnet stream;
- a marketable-only measurement protocol for West bottom-trawl samples in July–September 2022;
- a gillnet selectivity change from 2023 onward;
- eight implausible lengths generated by a millimetre/centimetre entry error;
- duplicate fish IDs introduced during a simulated merge.

These issues are retained in the raw simulated object and diagnosed explicitly. The analysis dataset is derived through transparent flags rather than by altering the original records.

## Main analytical components

1. Record-level plausibility and duplicate checks.
2. Sample-size diagnostics by year, month, area and gear.
3. Length-heaping diagnostics.
4. Annual 1-cm binned LFDs as the primary representation.
5. Kernel density and ridgeline-style displays as complementary visualizations.
6. Area × gear comparisons for recent years.
7. Median and interquartile trajectories by gear.
8. Length-class × year heatmaps.
9. Pooled versus equal-stratum standardized annual LFDs.
10. Sensitivity to incomplete measurement scope.
11. Jensen–Shannon divergence between consecutive annual distributions.

## Reuse with your own data

[`lfd_diagnostics.R`](lfd_diagnostics.R) separates the core diagnostics from the simulated example. It provides reusable functions to flag invalid/duplicate records, quantify sampling support across arbitrary strata, construct normalized LFDs, diagnose digit preference/heaping, compare two distributions using Jensen–Shannon divergence and a KS statistic, and test sensitivity to a known measurement-scope restriction.

The functions accept column names explicitly, so a user's data do not need to use this repository's simulated field names. A typical workflow is:

```r
source("03-length-frequency/lfd_diagnostics.R")

x <- flag_length_records(my_data, "length_cm", "fish_id",
                         min_length = 5, max_length = 100)
coverage <- lfd_coverage(x, c("year", "area", "fleet", "gear"),
                         id_col = "fish_id", min_n = 30)
lfd <- lfd_proportions(x[!x$length_out_of_range, ], "length_cm",
                       c("year", "area", "gear"), bin_width = 1)
heaping <- length_heaping(x, "length_cm", c("year", "gear"),
                          heaping_interval = 5)
```

Thresholds such as plausible length bounds and minimum sample size are intentionally supplied by the analyst: they are species-, programme- and question-dependent and should not be hidden defaults masquerading as universal fisheries rules.

## Important interpretation rule

A change in an LFD is not automatically a change in the population. It may instead reflect sampling coverage, fleet composition, gear selectivity, spatial redistribution, seasonal availability, protocol changes, market/sample selection, measurement rounding or other observation artefacts.

The module therefore stops before estimating recruitment, growth, mortality, selectivity or stock status.
